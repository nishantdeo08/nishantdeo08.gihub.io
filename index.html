I was introduced to radio astronomy about 3.5 years ago, and I've been hooked since. These are the topics I've worked on.

Long Period Transients (LPTs)

LPTs are radio sources that flash every few minutes to hours, far slower than ordinary pulsars. What powers them is still debated, with slowly spinning magnetars and white dwarf systems among the candidates. [Add your work here]

Fast Radio Bursts (FRBs)

FRBs are millisecond flashes of radio light from distant galaxies. Some repeat, most don't, and their origin remains open. They also act as probes of the gas between galaxies. [Add your work here]

Pulsars

Pulsars are fast-spinning neutron stars whose radio beams sweep past Earth like a lighthouse. Their precise timing lets us test gravity and study dense matter. [Add your work here]

The SPOTLIGHT project

SPOTLIGHT is a real-time, commensal survey at the GMRT that searches for FRBs, pulsars and LPTs while the telescope observes for other projects. It runs on a petaflop-scale GPU computing system and can localise bursts on the sky within seconds. [Add your role here]
